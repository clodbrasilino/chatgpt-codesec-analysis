Q1Is LLM code generation vulnerable? How vulnerable?

Full-dataset evidence, 974 tasks × 3 independent samples per model, multi-analyzer static gate (gcc-15 -fanalyzer, clang --analyze, Cppcheck Premium, flawfinder), max 10 healing rounds.

Yes — substantially. Between 34.4% (deepseek-v4-pro) and 38.3% (qwen-max) of first-shot generations contain defects detectable by static analysis alone, before any repair. Iterative healing with static feedback removes most of them (final clean 96.9–98.8%), but 90 and 36 samples respectively (3.1% / 1.2%) remain defective after 10 rounds. On the top-200 most vulnerability-prone tasks under the full static+dynamic gate, roughly 43–48% of initial samples are flagged.

Yes — behavior differs by model, and the effective knob is model-specific. For deepseek-v4-pro, low temperature (t0.2) is the significant lever (+12 pp initial cleanliness, p=0.0295). For qwen-max, the pattern reverses: t0.2 does almost nothing (+0.5 pp) while top_p 0.5 shows the larger start-cleanliness gain (+8.5 pp, not significant under the pre-registered sample-1 pairing, borderline under the all-samples sensitivity bar). Across all 6 grid cells, final clean rates stay ≥ 98% — conservative decoding never hurt repairability, and always numerically improved initial security. Model-level differences exist independently of decoding: qwen flags more initially (38.3% vs 34.4% full dataset) but retains fewer uncleaned samples (1.2% vs 3.1%).
Caveats (data-driven honesty): grid cells are 1-sample and were healed under the static gate; non-significance is inconclusive, not evidence of no effect. The definitive s+d versions (3 samples, shared round-0 via gen-source) are exactly what the running chains are producing now.
